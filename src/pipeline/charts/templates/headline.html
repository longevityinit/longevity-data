        Methodology and coverage

        Country-line faintness: opacity reflects each location’s share of the included countries’ total population in 2023, held constant across the timeline. A square-root scale keeps small populations visible, ranging from 6% opacity to 50% at a population share of 20% or more. This visual styling uses total population for both views; percentile calculations use sex-specific population.

        Percentiles: the selectable 10th, 25th, 75th, and 90th percentile lines rank countries and territories by life expectancy, then accumulate their female or male populations. Each line is the first country value reaching its selected share of the included population, with no interpolation. Women’s values are weighted by female population, and men’s values by male population. All sizes are included, and aggregates are excluded. Missing life expectancy is excluded and weights are renormalized over available observations. Values start in 1950. This describes country-level life expectancy, not percentiles of individual lifespans, nor the average within the bottom group. Large countries can produce jumps.

        This first version covers 1900–2023, at birth, separately for females and males. It excludes projections. Countries and territories are the export's locations with an OWID world-region assignment; regional and income aggregates are excluded from country calculations.

        Best practice: the highest value among countries and territories with total population strictly above one million in that year. Ties retain every winning location. World life expectancy: the sex-specific World series supplied by UN WPP through OWID, used directly without averaging country values.

        Income groups: the high- and low-income series supplied by UN WPP through OWID, used directly rather than averaging country life expectancies. Their classification vintage and membership methodology still require confirmation before publication.

        Missing values remain gaps; no interpolation is applied. Changing country coverage can affect the frontier. Before 1950, only a small, changing set of countries has observations: the World and income-group series therefore start in 1950. Historical population uses OWID’s current-border estimates, while HMD historical mortality coverage may differ; historical frontier eligibility needs further geographic review before publication. UK constituent areas without country IDs are excluded. This is a local first version; age 65 is not included.
