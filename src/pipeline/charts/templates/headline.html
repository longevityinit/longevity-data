    Our World in Data brings together the source datasets listed above. The Longevity Initiative calculates the Best practice and population-weighted percentile lines. Material produced by OWID is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Data from other providers are subject to their original licences.

    This chart shows life expectancy at birth for women and men, using historical estimates from 1751 to 2023. It does not include projections. Life expectancy describes how long someone would live if they experienced the death rates of that year throughout their life; it is not a prediction for a person born that year.

    Best practice is the highest life expectancy among countries and territories with a total population of more than one million that year. The line starts in 1850. Each year needs at least two eligible locations with data for the selected sex. If several locations tie, all are credited. A country without population data can still be shown on its own, but cannot contribute to Best practice.

    World life expectancy is the World series from the UN World Population Prospects, supplied through OWID. We use it directly rather than calculating an average of country values.

    Percentiles start in 1950. We sort countries and territories from lowest to highest life expectancy, then add up their populations until we reach the chosen share: 10%, 25%, 75%, or 90%. The line takes the life expectancy of the country where that share is reached, without interpolating between countries. Women’s lines use female population; men’s lines use male population. Countries of every size are included. Countries with missing data are left out, and the shares are calculated from the population with data available that year.

    These percentiles compare country-level life expectancy. They do not describe individual lifespans or the average for the bottom 10% or 25% of people. A large country can cause a jump in a percentile line.

    Coverage: Sweden provides the earliest observations, in 1751, and is the only country shown before 1800. Coverage remains limited and changes over time before 1950. The World series starts in 1950. Missing years are left as gaps; we do not fill in life expectancy or population values.

    Country calculations use locations with an OWID world-region assignment, excluding regional and income totals. UK constituent areas without country IDs are also excluded. Historical population estimates use present-day borders, which may differ from the territories covered by historical mortality records. We still need to check this match before publishing historical Best practice values. Changes in country coverage can also change the Best practice line.
