    Methodology and coverage

    Percentiles: the selectable 10th, 25th, 75th, and 90th percentile lines rank countries and territories by life expectancy, then accumulate their female or male populations. Each line is the first country value reaching its selected share of the included population, with no interpolation. Women’s values are weighted by female population, and men’s values by male population. All sizes are included, and aggregates are excluded. Missing life expectancy is excluded and weights are renormalized over available observations. Values start in 1950. This describes country-level life expectancy, not percentiles of individual lifespans, nor the average within the bottom group. Large countries can produce jumps.

    This first version covers 1751–2023, at birth, separately for females and males. It excludes projections. Countries and territories are the export's locations with an OWID world-region assignment; regional and income aggregates are excluded from country calculations.

    Best practice: the highest value among countries and territories with a recorded total population strictly above one million in that year. Best practice is shown from 1850 onward; at least two eligible locations with life expectancy observations are required for each year and sex. Country observations with missing population are still available individually but excluded from best practice; no population values are interpolated. Ties retain every winning location. World life expectancy: the sex-specific World series supplied by UN WPP through OWID, used directly without averaging country values.

    Missing values remain gaps; no interpolation is applied. Changing country coverage can affect the frontier. The earliest sex-specific observations are for Sweden in 1751; before 1800 Sweden is the only included location, and historical population is missing for most years. Before 1950, only a small, changing set of countries has observations: the World series therefore starts in 1950. Historical population uses OWID’s current-border estimates, while HMD historical mortality coverage may differ; historical frontier eligibility needs further geographic review before publication. UK constituent areas without country IDs are excluded. This is a local first version; age 65 is not included.
